Important: Do not connect the ESP32 until you have measured the LM2596 output and adjusted it to approximately 5.00 V. The 5V connection is the carrier board input, never the WROOM module's 3V3 pin. Never apply 12V to the ESP32, YX5200, sensor or LEDs.
USB programming: isolate the external ESP32 5V feed unless the carrier's USB/backfeed protection is verified. Do not power it from USB and the buck converter simultaneously with an unknown power path. Disconnect signals to unpowered peripherals to avoid phantom powering.

UART wires cross: ESP32 TX → YX5200 RX and ESP32 RX ← YX5200 TX. UART2 runs at 9600 baud, 8N1; confirm the module TX is 3.3V logic. GPIO labels are not physical header positions.
GPIO budget: 9 LED controls + 2 UART pins + 1 sensor = 12 application GPIOs. GPIO4, GPIO13 and GPIO14 remain unused output-capable candidates, subject to carrier wiring. GPIO1/3 remain reserved for serial/programming. GPIO21/22 are used by LEDs, not I²C. No expander or multiplexing is needed.
4. Six Common-Anode RGB LEDs — Three Pairs

Each traffic-light position contains two RGB LEDs. Both LEDs in a pair share the three color controls and always show the same color. The three pairs stay independent: 9 GPIOs, not 18. During normal traffic operation, only the selected pair is lit.

Common anode (CA): all six common legs connect to ESP32 3V3. Never connect them to GND or to 5V in this direct-GPIO circuit. Each color cathode goes through its own series resistor to its assigned GPIO. GPIO LOW = channel on; GPIO HIGH = channel off.

Parts count: 6 RGB LEDs, 18 × 470 Ω series resistors (one per physical LED/color channel), plus 9 × 10 kΩ recommended pull-ups (one per GPIO to 3V3). Pull-ups keep channels off before firmware initialization; they do not replace the series resistors. All three R/G/B channels are connected. Do not join color controls between different pairs.

Check the actual LED before connecting it

Reported label: LB E2-03-04-FCN RGB CA. Common-anode type is confirmed, but the exact datasheet and physical leg order are unverified. Identify the common and R/G/B legs from its datasheet or a diode test; do not infer polarity or color order from leg length alone.

470 Ω is a conservative starting value, not a verified part-specific specification. For a 2.0V channel, ideal current is (3.3 − 2.0) / 470 ≈ 2.8mA per LED, or 5.6mA per shared GPIO. Measure actual channel currents and check the carrier's 3.3V regulator capacity. Red/green brightness balance determines the yellow hue; increase a channel's resistor value if needed to reduce its brightness.

Green/blue channels may be dim if their forward voltage is too close to 3.3V. If a higher LED supply or more brightness is required, use suitable external current-sinking drivers and revise this wiring. Do not move the common anodes to 5V while the cathodes remain connected to ESP32 GPIOs.
5. High-Five Sensor — Cherry DB1 Mechanical Switch

Endstop logic: released = HIGH, pressed = LOW. Firmware uses INPUT_PULLUP and a 30ms debounce; a held switch does not retrigger. Pressing the switch closes COM–NO, connecting GPIO27 to GND. The internal pull-up holds GPIO27 HIGH when released; no separate power wire is needed. 
Inside ESP32:
3V3 -- internal pull-up -- GPIO27 -- NO / 4   o/ o   COM / 1 -- GND
                                      DB1 contact closes on press

NC / 2: unused; insulate this terminal.
No 3V3, 5V or 12V wire goes to the DB1 switch. It is a bare mechanical contact, not a powered VCC/GND/SIGNAL module. With COM connected to GND, connecting 3V3 to NC would short the supply while released. Keep NC disconnected; using NC for the signal would reverse the current firmware's behavior.

Use the molded COM/NO/NC or 1/4/2 markings, not a guessed left-to-right pin order. With the switch disconnected and all power off, use continuity mode: COM–NO is open when released and closed when pressed; COM–NC does the opposite. Verify repeated presses in SENSOR TEST. The DB1's published power-switch ratings do not by themselves guarantee reliable operation at the tiny GPIO pull-up current.

Sources: Cherry DB-series datasheet (page 1 identifies NC, NO and Common) and ZF terminal definitions (COM = 1, NC = 2, NO = 4).

8. Sequence and Prepared SD Card

Red: 3 seconds → yellow: 1 second → green: wait for high-five → reward music + RGB animation: 30 seconds → red.

The reward timer starts at the high-five. At the deadline, firmware stops the animation and queues an audio stop; audible timing includes UART/module latency. Manual AUDIO TEST playback and boot/error sounds do not have this reward timeout. Animation rotates red, green and blue across the three pairs every 80ms; both LEDs in each pair match.

9. Power-Off Wiring and First-Test Checklist
  1. With peripherals disconnected, verify adapter polarity and set the buck to 5.00V. Power off before making connections; never route amplifier power through a breadboard.
  2. Flash the paired-RGB firmware. Use the verified USB/external-power arrangement; old single-color firmware does not match this active-low nine-channel wiring.
  3. Open the 115200-baud serial monitor and reset. Send a mode number followed by Enter within 5 seconds; otherwise FULL starts automatically.
  4. Choose 2 — LIGHTS TEST: verify top red, middle yellow, bottom green, off, cycle, and dance. Check both LEDs in each pair, all nine channels, reset-off behavior, resistor currents, yellow balance and blue/green brightness.
  5. With power off, connect DB1 COM/1 to GND and NO/4 to GPIO27; leave NC/2 unused. Choose 4 — SENSOR TEST: confirm released HIGH, pressed LOW and one event per press/release. Choose 3 — AUDIO TEST: verify initialization, then boot, error, play 1 and stop.
  6. Reboot into 1 — FULL and verify the 3s / 1s / high-five / 30s sequence. Measure supply voltages under load and check for overheating, resets and loose joints.
Physical commissioning is still pending. A successful build and automated tests do not verify the LED pinout, actual brightness, audio output or power wiring. Use the README and hardware commissioning record for the full checks.
